Case study 01 · Skincare & beauty · Spec brief

One pump, real acne.

A UGC brief for Tower 28 Swipe Serum Foundation, built on what their ads weren't showing: real acne getting covered, not skin that never needed it.

01 · The opportunity

The before/after nobody believes

I went through the product and the brand's existing ads and found an opportunity they hadn't used: showing real, visible acne in the before/after.

In the reference ad, the creator already has great skin, so there's barely any change to see. That's common in foundation before/afters, and it can stop people from buying: if you can't see the difference, you can't picture it on your own skin.

If women with acne watch it get covered with one pump and no filters, they'll stop and watch.

02 · Research

Every foundation costs something

The same frustration kept coming up: the foundations that cover acne bring a new problem with them, and the ones that feel good don't cover.

CakeyFull coverage settles into pores and texture
PatchyLight formulas leave redness showing
DryingMatte finishes last but dry the skin out
A breakout, a day laterThe cost you can't see at the counter

The problem (UMP): most foundations are built to do one thing well. Cover, feel light, last or be gentle. Each is good at one and bad at the rest, so every new bottle fixes one problem and brings back another.

The mechanism (UMS): a serum foundation that covers without the heavy feel. No fragrance, no silicones, no hyaluronic acid. Portulaca extract to calm irritation, fermented oat to support the skin barrier, and buildable coverage: one pump covers, two or three cover more.

Read the full UMP / UMS

UMP. If every foundation you've tried either covers but looks cakey, or feels light but doesn't cover, think about the last one you tried. It either looked great for an hour, or covered well but felt heavy, and the reason is that most foundations are built to do one thing well: cover, or feel light, or last, or be gentle. Full coverage covers the acne but settles into pores and fine lines, so you hide the redness and show the texture. Go light and it doesn't cover, and what's left looks patchy. A matte finish holds but dries you out, and a glowy one is gone by hour two or three. And the breakout shows up a day or two later, a cost you can't judge at the counter because it hasn't happened yet. Adding a primer, a powder or a concealer just moves the cost somewhere else, and switching brands does the same. You wanted coverage, comfort, wear time and skin that isn't worse the next day, and no foundation gave you all four.

UMS. Swipe Serum Foundation doesn't make you choose. It covers without the heavy feel, with no fragrance and no silicones. Start with one pump over moisturizer: one pump covers, two to three cover more, so you pick the coverage and keep the feel. It glides on like a serum. Portulaca extract helps calm irritation and keep breakout-causing bacteria in check, and fermented oat hydrates and strengthens the skin barrier so skin doesn't feel dry underneath. Medium coverage you can build on a bad breakout day, made to last through the day.

Notes for the creator and editor

  • Video reference: view the original ad on Atria ↗
  • The creator needs visible acne, filmed in daylight. Real skin is the whole point of the ad.
  • The difference has to show after the first pump, so the before/after reads instantly.
  • Keep the split screen the same across all three hooks so only the opening changes.
  • Deliverables: 9:16, 20–30 seconds, one cut per hook.
05 · Test plan

How we'd know

Hypothesis: women with acne-prone skin will stop for a demo on skin like theirs, and a visible one-pump result will beat the brand's current clear-skin before/after.

What we test

  • 3 hooks × the same body and visuals
  • Against the brand's current before/after ad as control

What we watch

  • Hook rate: does the opening stop the scroll?
  • Hold rate: do people stay for the demo?
  • CTR and CPA vs. control

What comes next

  • Hook wins: brief new bodies around it, e.g. a bad-breakout day with 2–3 pumps
  • Strong hook, weak clicks: the demo or offer needs work, not the opening
  • Weak hook rate: test the same demo as a static before/after
